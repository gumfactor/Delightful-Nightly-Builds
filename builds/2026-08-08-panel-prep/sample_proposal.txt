# Specific Aims

Empathic accuracy failures are a core feature of several forensic-relevant
conditions, yet the neural mechanisms that distinguish a failure of
perspective-taking from a failure of emotional resonance remain unknown.
Our central hypothesis is that these two failure modes are dissociable at
the level of anterior insula and temporoparietal junction connectivity.

Aim 1: Determine whether resting-state connectivity between anterior insula
and TPJ predicts empathic accuracy on a validated behavioral task.

Aim 2: Test whether experimentally manipulating attention to internal
bodily states selectively impairs affective, but not cognitive, empathy.

Upon completion of these aims, this work will provide the first mechanistic
account of empathy subtype dissociation, with direct relevance to forensic
risk assessment protocols.

# Significance

A critical barrier to progress in this field is that empathy is treated as
a unitary construct in most clinical assessment tools, despite two decades
of behavioral evidence that cognitive and affective empathy dissociate.
It is unclear whether this dissociation has a distinct neural signature,
and recent advances in high-resolution 7T imaging make this question newly
tractable, which is why this work is timely.

# Innovation

This proposal is innovative in two respects: it is the first to combine
an interoceptive-attention manipulation with simultaneous connectivity
mapping, and it departs from prior unitary-construct models of empathy by
testing a dissociable, two-pathway account directly.

# Approach

A power analysis using G*Power (two-tailed, alpha=.05, power=.90) indicates
a sample size of 120 participants is required to detect the hypothesized
effect size of d=0.45.

Timeline: Year 1 will focus on participant recruitment and piloting the
interoceptive-attention manipulation; Year 2 will complete data collection;
Year 3 will focus on analysis and dissemination.

Potential pitfalls include low compliance with the attention manipulation;
alternative approaches include a passive-viewing control condition if
active manipulation proves infeasible.

Preliminary data from our lab show a reliable insula-TPJ connectivity
signal in a pilot sample of 18 participants, supporting feasibility.

Data will be analyzed using mixed-effects regression models with
participant as a random effect, and ANOVA for the manipulation-check
analyses.

# Rigor and Reproducibility

Sex as a biological variable will be considered in all analyses, with sex
included as a covariate and examined for interaction effects.

All raters scoring behavioral responses will be blinded to condition, and
condition order will be randomized across participants.

All antibodies and reagents used in the physiological data collection
pipeline will be authenticated against RRID identifiers before use.

We will assess reproducibility of the primary connectivity finding in an
independent held-out subsample collected in Year 3.
