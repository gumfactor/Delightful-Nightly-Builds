# Affective Empathy Deficits and Recidivism Risk in a Forensic Sample

Abstract: Prior work on empathy and psychopathic traits has often relied on
a single combined empathy score, obscuring a well-documented dissociation
between affective empathy (the capacity to share another's emotional state)
and cognitive empathy (the capacity to represent another's mental state
without necessarily sharing it). We administered a validated affective
empathy scale alongside a separate cognitive perspective-taking task to a
forensic sample, and used a behavioral reaction-time task as an implicit
measure to triangulate against self-report, given known self-report
distortion issues in forensic populations.

Affective empathy deficits, but not cognitive empathy scores, independently
predicted recidivism risk after controlling for standard risk factors. Stress
reactivity — measured via cortisol reactivity to a lab stressor — moderated
this relationship: participants with both low affective empathy and blunted
stress reactivity showed the highest predicted risk, consistent with a
broader affective neuroscience account in which empathy deficits and stress
dysregulation share a common mechanistic pathway rather than operating
independently.

These findings support assessment protocols that separate affective and
cognitive empathy rather than combining them, and suggest stress reactivity
measures may add incremental predictive value in forensic risk assessment.
