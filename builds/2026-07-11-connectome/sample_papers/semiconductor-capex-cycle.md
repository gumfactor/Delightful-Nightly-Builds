# Capital Allocation Across the AI Infrastructure Semiconductor Capex Cycle

Abstract: We model hyperscaler capital expenditure guidance as a leading
indicator for downstream semiconductor demand across three layers of the AI
infrastructure stack: compute, memory/interconnect, and power/cooling. Using
quarterly capex revisions from the largest cloud infrastructure operators as
the primary signal, we find that upward guidance revisions propagate through
the chain with a measurable lag — compute-layer demand responds fastest,
memory pricing cycles respond with the longest and most volatile lag, and
power/cooling capacity constraints emerge only after compute and memory
capacity has already scaled.

This staged propagation matters for capital allocation: capex guidance
revisions are a genuine leading indicator, but the layer that benefits shifts
over the course of a single buildout cycle rather than remaining fixed. Power
and cooling infrastructure, historically treated as a secondary
consideration behind compute and memory, become the binding constraint once
data center scale itself is the limiting factor — a pattern consistent with
recent capacity announcements across the sector.

We recommend that capex-cycle theses explicitly track guidance revisions,
memory pricing, and power/cooling capacity as three separate signals rather
than treating "AI infrastructure spend" as a single undifferentiated trend.
