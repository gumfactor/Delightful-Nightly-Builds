# Regulators Push Back on "Canadian-Owned" Labels After Franchise Ownership Case

A recent enforcement case has renewed scrutiny of how companies get labeled
"Canadian-owned" in public directories and procurement databases. The
dispute centered on a franchise brand whose parent company is foreign-owned
while the individual franchise locations are operated as Canadian small
businesses — exactly the kind of edge case that a simple headquarters-address
lookup gets wrong.

Industry groups are now pushing for classification standards that require a
control-block test — identifying who actually holds the largest voting
stake — rather than accepting "headquartered in Canada" or "listed on a
Canadian exchange" as sufficient evidence of Canadian ownership on their own.
Several directories have also faced criticism for presenting ownership
labels as static facts rather than time-stamped, confidence-rated
assessments; ownership structures change through acquisitions and public
offerings, and a stale "Canadian-owned" label left unchanged for years is
arguably worse than an explicit "uncertain, last verified" flag.

The case is expected to influence how ownership classification databases —
not just for Canadian companies, but for any public registry built from
structured public-record data — handle provenance and confidence going
forward.
