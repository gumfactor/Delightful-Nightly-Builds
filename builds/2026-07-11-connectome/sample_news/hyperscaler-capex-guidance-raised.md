# Hyperscalers Raise Capex Guidance Again, Power and Cooling Now the Bottleneck

Three of the largest cloud infrastructure operators raised full-year capital
expenditure guidance this week, extending a string of upward revisions tied
to AI infrastructure buildout. Compute-layer spending was the first mover in
this cycle and remains elevated, but analysts on the earnings calls kept
circling back to a different constraint: power and cooling capacity, not
chip supply, is now the binding constraint on how fast new data center
capacity can come online.

Memory pricing, historically the most cyclical and hardest-to-time segment
of the semiconductor supply chain, ticked up again this quarter as
memory-layer demand catches up to the compute buildout that preceded it by
several quarters. One portfolio manager described the pattern as "capex
guidance revisions propagating down the chain in stages — compute first,
memory with a lag, and now power and cooling as the layer nobody priced in
early enough."

Power and cooling infrastructure spend, once treated as a secondary
line item behind compute and memory, is increasingly described by industry
participants as the "picks and shovels behind the picks and shovels" for
this capex cycle — worth tracking as its own signal rather than folding it
into a single undifferentiated "AI infrastructure spend" narrative.
